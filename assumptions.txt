assumptions.txt
================
This file lists the decisions we had to make ourselves, because the
assignment document didn't give clear rules for these parts. For each one:
what the document doesn't say (the problem), and what we picked instead
(what we did), and why. Grouped by file, in the order they were written.

This is different from gapsFilled.txt (things that were broken and got
fixed later) and yetToMake.txt (things we haven't built at all).


board.c
=========

1. Railway purchase price LKR 1,500 / mortgage value LKR 750
Problem: Appendix B only gives prices for the 8 colour groups. Table 2 only
gives railway RENT, not a price to buy or mortgage a station.
What we did: mortgage value = 50% of purchase price, same as every colour
group already uses. Purchase price set to LKR 1,500, same as utilities
(#2), since both are similar - no colour group, and simple flat/dice-based
income - with no price given anywhere.

2. Utility purchase price LKR 1,500 / mortgage value LKR 750
Problem: Section 1.1.3 only gives the rent formula for utilities, no
purchase price.
What we did: same 50% mortgage rule as #1. Purchase price matched to the
cheapest colour group (Brown), since utility rent is capped by a dice roll
instead of a big group-based table - so it gets the lowest price too.


finance.c
===========

3. payRent() stops rent at whatever cash the payer has
Problem: Rule 7 doesn't say what happens if a player can't afford full rent.
What we did: letting cash go negative would break other math (net worth,
loans, auctions). Making a player bankrupt right inside payRent() was also
rejected, because Rule 14 checks a player's WHOLE financial picture, not
just one rent payment. So rent is simply capped at whatever cash the player
has, and the real bankruptcy check happens separately in checkBankruptcy()
(#6).

4. calculateNetWorth() subtracts mortgageValue as a debt
Problem: Rule 15's exact net worth formula doesn't mention "mortgage" as
one of its terms, but an earlier, looser description on page 2 does.
What we did: without subtracting it, a player could raise their net worth
just by mortgaging everything and keeping the cash, while still counting
the full property value too. Subtracting mortgageValue avoids that, the
same way an existing loan is already subtracted elsewhere.

5. currentValue is one single number covering "Property/Building/Railway/
   Utility Value"
Problem: Rule 15 lists 4 separate value terms, but the Square struct only
has one currentValue field.
What we did: adding 3 new fields just to match the wording wasn't worth it,
since nothing else would use them. One number per square works the same
way for every square type. Note: "Building Value" isn't really tracked at
all, since nothing raises currentValue when a house or hotel gets built
(this is listed in yetToMake.txt).

6. checkBankruptcy() declares bankruptcy at net worth <= 0, and gives every
   square back to the Bank
Problem: Rule 14 says "bankruptcy rules defined later," but no such section
actually exists anywhere in the document. Both the exact trigger and what
happens to the properties are left undefined.
What we did: net worth <= 0 already matches "liabilities exceed assets," so
no new formula was needed. Properties go back to the Bank the same way
Rule-LK 6 already does for foreclosed loans, since there's no rule
describing how to split them between other players instead.


events.c
==========

7. Heavy Floods' "coastal" property reuses the same 8-square pool as #9/#10,
   plus Jaffna Town and Trincomalee
Problem: Appendix A's Heavy Floods card doesn't say which squares count as
"coastal."
What we did: two other cards already name exact squares for "coastal"
(#9, #10) - Galle Fort/Unawatuna/Hikkaduwa and Bambalapitiya/Wellawatte/
Mount Lavinia - so those 6 are reused here too, so "coastal" means the same
thing everywhere in the game, not a different guess per card. User-directed
addition: Jaffna Town and Trincomalee are also genuinely coastal Sri Lankan
cities, so they were added to this same pool - Nallur was deliberately left
out, since it's a suburb of Jaffna rather than a coastal city itself, and
the document only ever names it alongside Jaffna Town/Trincomalee for a
different, unrelated effect (Northern Development Programme, Table 4).

8. Foreign Funding's "commercial property values" = every property square
   on the board
Problem: Appendix A never defines a board category called "commercial."
What we did: Bank/Insurance/Utility squares don't even have a value that
can go up, so a literal reading would do nothing. Applied to every regular
property square instead, since those are the only ones that can actually
gain value.

9. Beach Pollution's "Southern coastal rents" = Galle Fort, Unawatuna,
   Hikkaduwa (Yellow group)
Problem: Table 4 doesn't list which squares this affects.
What we did: another card in the same table, Southern Tourism Boom, names
these exact 3 squares as "Southern." Read as the same region having a good
swing at one point and a bad swing at another, not two unrelated sets.

10. Flood Damage's "low-lying coastal" = Bambalapitiya, Wellawatte, Mount
    Lavinia (Light Blue group)
Problem: Table 4 gives no square list here either, and the wording is
different from Beach Pollution, which suggests a different area.
What we did: these are real, well-known flood-prone suburbs of Colombo,
and a group different from the Yellow group #9 already used.

11. Water Shortage's "surrounding properties" = Unawatuna (27) and
    Hikkaduwa (29)
Problem: Table 4 doesn't say which properties "surround" the water utility
(square 28).
What we did: read this literally by board position, not by theme - these
are simply the two squares sitting right next to square 28.

12. Luxury Property Tax charges every round it's active, not just once
Problem: Rule-LK 24 calls it "annual" but doesn't say how many times to
charge it or when to stop.
What we did: charges 25% of value every round while active, for 20 rounds
(GOVERNMENT_REGULATION_DURATION_ROUNDS) - the same length every other
regulation already uses.

13. Disaster repair cost changes by disaster type, and includes any
    houses/hotel on the property
Problem: Rule-LK 10 never gives a repair cost formula, and doesn't say
whether some disasters are worse than others.
What we did: Electrical Failure 5%, Riot 10%, Flood 15%, Fire 20%, Building
Collapse 30% (30% reuses DEPRECIATION_MAX_PERCENTAGE, the document's own
"worst damage" number, for the worst disaster). This percentage is taken
from the property's value PLUS its houses/hotel replacement cost, not the
property value alone, since a disaster on a developed property should cost
more to fix. The insurance payout percentages (80%/100%) didn't change -
they just multiply this new repair cost.

14. Business Interruption Insurance is treated as covering every disaster
    type (hotels only)
Problem: unlike the other two policies, it doesn't list which disasters it
protects against.
What we did: read this as on purpose - this policy protects the hotel's
income, not one specific disaster, so it makes sense for it to cover
anything.

15. Building Collapse and Electrical Failure aren't covered by either Basic
    or Comprehensive Insurance
Problem: Rule-LK 10 lists these as real disasters, but neither policy's
coverage list mentions them.
What we did: implemented this exactly as written, instead of guessing it
was a mistake and adding them to Comprehensive "just in case."

16. A Government Regulation stays active for 20 rounds (the same as its
    draw interval)
Problem: Rule-LK 24 says a new regulation is picked every 20 rounds, but
never says how long that regulation actually lasts.
What we did: matched how Regional Cards (15 rounds) and National Cards
already work - active for exactly as long as the gap between draws, to
keep all the periodic systems working the same way.

17. Both disaster type and regulation type are picked with equal chance
Problem: both rules just say "randomly," with no percentages given.
What we did: equal chance for every option is the simplest choice with no
extra guessing. Picking, say, "Fire happens more often than Flood" would
be a much bigger invented rule with nothing in the document to support it.


players.c
===========

18. Conservative Banker's "bankruptcy imminent" = cash below 5% of
    STARTING_CASH (LKR 1,500)
Problem: Section 3.2 says "unless imminent" but gives no number.
What we did: tied the number to STARTING_CASH, the one clear number the
whole document is built around. 5% keeps this close to real desperation,
matching this strategy's cautious personality. This same number is reused
as a general "cash is dangerously low" signal in decideMortgage() (#23).

19. Opportunistic Trader's "return exceeds borrowing cost" = current rent
    income compared to one round of loan interest
Problem: Section 3.4 uses both terms but never defines either one, and
there's no system anywhere that predicts future income.
What we did: used the player's EXISTING total rent income as a stand-in
for "expected return." For "borrowing cost," reused the same interest
formula finance.c already uses everywhere else.

20. Aggressive Investor's "money that increases rental income" = a
    monopoly with room left to build
Problem: Section 3.1 gives no formula for predicting future rent.
What we did: the only real rule connecting money to more rent is building
houses/hotels on a completed monopoly (Table 6). So this was read as
"do I own a monopoly with room left to build."

21. Risk Taker's "insures only after a loss" = "only once the property is
    currently damaged"
Problem: the literal wording means remembering a loss even after it's
already been repaired.
What we did: tracking a property's full damage history forever would need
a brand-new field just for this one line of flavour text. Reused the
existing isDamaged flag instead - it's not a perfect match for "after a
loss," but it keeps the same "waits until something goes wrong" idea
without adding new data just for this.

22. Opportunistic Trader's "high-value developments" = a hotel
Problem: no number is given anywhere for what counts as "high-value."
What we did: a hotel is already the clearest "most developed" marker in
the game (the top rent tier in Table 6), so no made-up LKR cutoff was
needed.

23. decideMortgage()'s trigger and target choice was fully made up
Problem: Section 3 never mentions mortgaging a property for any strategy.
What we did: made it the same for all 4 strategies - only mortgage as a
last resort, using #18's cash-danger line, instead of inventing 4
different unsupported personalities. Picks the eligible square with the
highest mortgage value, since that raises the most cash from one property.

24. Conservative Banker's "sufficient funds" for loan repayment = any cash
    above 0
Problem: "sufficient" isn't defined as "the whole loan" or "just some of
it."
What we did: Rule-LK 5 clearly allows paying back only part of a loan, so
this always asks to repay the full amount, and lets repayLoan()'s own
cash-limit naturally turn that into a partial payment when there isn't
enough cash.

25. Conservative/Opportunistic auction bids accept a bid that equals market
    value, not just below it
Problem/context: Section 3.2/3.4 say bidding "below" market value or at a
"discount," which suggests a bid exactly equal to market value should be
turned down.
What we did: this was originally coded as a strict "less than" check, then
simplified to "less than or equal to" at the user's direct request, to
keep the code simpler even though it's a tiny bit less exact to the word
"below." Written down here so it's a visible, on-purpose choice, not a
silent mistake.


Added after the first full pass, then made simpler for the viva
================================================================================
Entries 26 onward were added once the remaining gaps in yetToMake.txt
(round-tick systems, railway/utility rent, Income Tax, Economic Events,
Rule-LK 36) were filled in. Some of these entries used to describe more
complicated logic - especially an exact "undo the percentage change" system
for Rule-LK 35, and an Economic Events deck guessed only from card names
before the real assignment text was available. Both were made simpler
afterward so they're easy to explain in a viva. The entries below describe
the CURRENT code. Kept as its own section instead of renumbering 1-25
above, so "(see #18)" style references still point to the right entry.

finance.c
===========

26. Income Tax = 15% of current cash (INCOME_TAX_STARTING_PERCENTAGE)
Problem: Rule 11 says to collect tax on landing but gives no amount or
rate.
What we did: following the lecturer's clarification, this starts as a 15%
rate instead of a flat number. Cash (not net worth or property value) was
picked as the base so this tax stays clearly different from the Community
Development Fund tax (#42), which the lecturer said should use property
value - using the same base for both would make them the same tax twice.
Stored as a field that can change, so the "Increase Property Tax"
regulation (+50%) has something real to adjust.

27. depreciateProperties() uses a simple round-count check and a running
    percentage counter
Problem: Rule-LK 15/16 give the 1%-every-5-rounds/30%-cap rule, but not how
to detect "every 5 rounds" in code, or how to remember how much has
already been cut.
What we did: propertyAge % 5 == 0 is the simplest possible check for
"every 5 rounds." A counter field (depreciationPercent) adds 1% each time
a cut happens and stops once it reaches the 30% cap - easy to explain in
one sentence. Each cut is taken off whatever currentValue is AT THAT
MOMENT, so it stacks properly with anything inflation/cards already did to
it.

28. Railway and utility rent are worked out fresh every time rent is paid
Problem: Table 2 and Section 1.1.3 both describe rent as something worked
out live at the moment of payment, not a fixed number the way property
rent is.
What we did: since no card ever targets railway/utility rent directly,
there's nothing that needs a stored, ongoing number to build on top of.
calculateRailwayRent()/calculateUtilityRent() just work out the right
number straight from the rules every single time - simple, and nothing to
keep in sync.

29. Starting loan interest rate = Table 9's "Stable Economy" rate (8%)
Problem: Rule-LK 2-7 explain how loan interest builds up, but never say
what rate a loan starts at, and nothing ties Table 9's 5 named conditions
to any specific point in the game.
What we did: rather than building a whole extra system to track "which
economic condition are we in right now," the game just starts on the row
every game naturally begins on - Stable Economy, 8% - set once at the
start, and lets inflation/cards/regulations move it from there.

30. retryDamageRepairs() tells disaster damage apart from a Political
    Rally closure using closedRoundsLeft, not a new flag
Problem: Rule-LK 11 says repairs happen "automatically" once the owner can
afford it, but the first version of the code only tried once, right when
the disaster happened. Adding a retry created a new issue: Political Rally
(#31) also uses the same "damaged" flag, but shouldn't cost anything to
undo - it should just reopen for free.
What we did: closedRoundsLeft is only ever set for a Political Rally, never
for real disaster damage. So the repair-retry logic only tries a paid
repair when isDamaged is true AND closedRoundsLeft is 0, leaving Rally
closures to reopen on their own for free.


events.c
==========

31. Political Rally reuses the isDamaged flag to mean "closed"
Problem: Appendix A's Political Rally closes a property for 2 rounds - a
different idea from disaster damage - but the Square struct only had one
yes/no field for "can't collect rent."
What we did: "closed" and "damaged" behave exactly the same everywhere
else in the code (no rent either way), so a second flag would just repeat
every isDamaged check for no real reason. Political Rally sets isDamaged
to true just like a disaster does, plus sets closedRoundsLeft to 2. That
countdown clears isDamaged for free once it reaches 0, with no repair cost
involved.

32. Insurance Discount gives a -20% discount on every premium, with no
    timer of its own
Problem: Appendix A's card doesn't say how long the discount lasts, or
whether it applies to everyone or just the player who drew it.
What we did: reused the same field the Insurance Regulation already uses
(economy.insurancePremiumDiscountPercent), instead of adding a separate,
player-only field just for this card. Because of that, it only turns off
as a side effect of a Government Regulation's own timer running out (see
#33) - a small, accepted inconsistency.

33. Regional Cards, Government Regulations, and Market Boom/Decline change
    prices/rent FOREVER - none of them undo the change afterward
Problem: Rule-LK 35 says a Regional Card's effect should "return to
normal" after 15 rounds, and Rule-LK 31/32 suggest the same for Market
Boom/Decline. But there's no formula anywhere for undoing a stacked
percentage change, and several effects can already be layered on the same
square at once (Rule-LK 34).
What we did: an earlier version tracked and exactly undid each effect, but
it needed a shared "remember what to undo" system and careful division for
around a dozen differently-shaped effects - too much to explain clearly in
a viva. Simplified so changes just stay in place forever, the same way
Inflation and National Event Cards already work. The countdown timer
itself still works correctly (so the on-screen display and next card draw
are correct) - only the actual price change is left as it is. This trades
a bit of accuracy for code that's much easier to explain.

34. The 30-round "can't pick this group again" cooldown (Rule-LK 33) starts
    counting from the moment a group is picked, not from when the
    boom/decline ends
Problem: Rule-LK 33 doesn't say exactly when this 30-round window starts.
What we did: this is the simplest option - it's set on the very same line
the group gets picked, no extra "this effect just ended" step needed
(which, as explained in #33, doesn't exist anyway). Since the boom/decline
itself only lasts 10 of those 30 rounds, the real-world difference is
small either way.

35. The Economic Events deck (Rule-LK 18) skips 3 small effects that give
    no number to use
Problem: out of Rule-LK 18's roughly 14 listed effects, 3 don't give any
number at all: Heavy Monsoon's "Flood risk increases," and Political
Unrest's "Riot probability doubles" and "Business interruption claims
increases."
What we did: rather than inventing a fake "probability" system for these
three, they're simply left out, with a short comment explaining why. Every
effect that DOES give a real number is implemented exactly as written.

36. Rule-LK 36's "current market conditions" display prints once at the
    end of every round
Problem: the rule just says to "display" this information, without saying
how often.
What we did: since the whole simulation runs with no player input at all,
there's no natural moment for an "on request" display. It's printed once
per round instead, right next to the existing round summary.


players.c
===========

37. The Anti-Speculation Act's "3 undeveloped properties" only counts
    normal property squares with 0 houses and no hotel
Problem: Rule-LK 24 doesn't clearly say whether railways/utilities (which
can never be built on) or mortgaged properties should count toward this
limit.
What we did: railways/utilities are left out, since "undeveloped" should
mean something that COULD be developed but currently isn't. Mortgaged
properties still count, since mortgaging a property doesn't change whether
it's being "land-banked." This check sits at the very start of the
purchase decision, so it applies the same way no matter which strategy is
buying.


game.c
========

38. Bankrupt players are left out of the check that decides when a round
    is finished
Problem: a round finishes when the SLOWEST solvent player has gone all the
way around the board once (not after a fixed number of turns) - but once a
player goes bankrupt, their own "how far around the board" counter freezes
forever, since their turns stop doing anything.
What we did: if bankrupt players were still included in this check, their
frozen counter would become the permanent lowest number, and round-end
effects (interest, inflation, disasters, every card) would simply stop
happening for the rest of the game. So they're left out of this one check,
matching how they're already ignored everywhere else once bankrupt.

39. The "has a round finished" check runs after every single turn, not
    once every 4 turns
Problem: since a round now means "the slowest player finished a lap," that
moment can happen on any player's turn, not always the same one.
What we did: checking only once every 4 turns could trigger round-end
effects a whole cycle too late. Checking after every turn instead catches
the exact moment a round actually finishes. One side effect worth
mentioning: many more individual turns are now needed to reach the
500-round limit, since a "round" now means a full lap of the board instead
of just 4 turns - this is the correct, expected result of rounds meaning
real progress now.


board.c (continued)
======================

40. Every property's price/rent is typed directly into the code from
    Rent.csv, not read from the file while the game runs
Problem: Rent.csv gives a different price/rent for each individual
property, which is more detailed than Appendix B's one-price-per-group
table.
What we did: a working file-reading version was built first, but reading
files, splitting up each line by hand, and matching names back to squares
was a lot of moving parts to explain confidently in a viva, for a file of
only 22 rows that never changes while the game is running. It was replaced
by simply typing the same 22 numbers straight into the code, the same way
houseCost/hotelCost/mortgageValue already are. Rent.csv stays in the
project only as the source those numbers were copied from.


players.c (continued)
========================

41. decidePayOffMortgage() is the exact opposite of decideMortgage() (#23)
Problem: same as mortgaging itself - Section 3 never mentions paying a
mortgage back for any strategy.
What we did: pays off a mortgage once the player's cash would still stay
above the same danger line from #18/#23 after paying. Simple to say out
loud: "mortgage when broke, pay it back once it's safe to." The cheapest
mortgaged property gets paid off first (the opposite of #23's "highest
first"), since that brings back a rent-earning property for the least
money. The payoff amount is exactly the mortgage value, with nothing extra
added on top, since the document gives no number for any extra charge.

Entries 42 onward cover decisions found while double-checking
decideToMaintain(), decideRenovateStructuralDamage(), decideLoanExtension(),

finance.c
===========

42. Community Development Fund tax = 10% of total property value only (no
    railways, utilities, or buildings)
Problem: the lecturer's clarification says "10% of total assets... current
market rate of only the property (not buildings)," but doesn't match
"property" to the board's actual square types.
What we did: read "property" as exactly Table 1's Property category,
leaving out railways/utilities. "Not buildings" needed no extra work,
since currentValue was already one combined number with no separate
building part (#5). Kept on its own field, separate from Income Tax (#26),
so the two taxes never use the same number.

43. Taking a new loan, or increasing one, pledges every eligible property
    the player owns, not a chosen few
Problem: Rule-LK 3 describes what a "Loan Locked" property is, but never
says how a player picks WHICH properties to pledge for a given loan.
What we did: pledges every unmortgaged, unlocked property the player owns
- the exact same set calculateMaxLoan() already adds up to work out the
maximum loan. This is the simplest possible rule ("pledge everything that
qualifies"), instead of inventing a made-up way to pick only some of them.

players.c
===========

44. decideToMaintain() is the same for 3 of the 4 strategies, but Risk
    Taker copies his depreciation-neglect trait
Problem: Rule-LK 27 explains how maintenance works, but no strategy in
Section 3 mentions maintenance directly.
What we did: Aggressive/Conservative/Opportunistic all share one rule - fix
the worst-condition building they can afford, same idea as #23. Risk
Taker reuses his stated trait "ignores depreciation until repair becomes
unavoidable" (written for a different but similar mechanic) for
maintenance too: he only fixes a building once its condition drops to 25%
(the point where Table 3 says the building closes), instead of at any
condition below 100% like the others.

45. decideRenovateStructuralDamage() is the same for all 4 strategies:
    fixes the first damaged property it can afford
Problem: Rule-LK 29 explains how this renovation works, but no strategy
mentions structural damage at all.
What we did: same reasoning as #44 - just picks the first eligible square
the player can afford the 25% replacement cost for. No strategy split was
made here, since structural damage is just a yes/no flag, not a number
that can be delayed against like building condition can.

46. decideRenovateDepreciation()'s Risk Taker (waits for the 30% cap) and
    Aggressive (renovates at any amount) thresholds are both made up
Problem: Section 3.2/3.4 give exact numbers for Conservative (>10%) and
Opportunistic (>15%), but Risk Taker and Aggressive have no stated number.
What we did: Risk Taker's "ignores depreciation until repair becomes
unavoidable" is read as waiting for the full 30% cap
(DEPRECIATION_MAX_PERCENTAGE). Aggressive has no matching line at all, so
it renovates the moment any depreciation appears, matching its rent-first
focus.

47. decideLoanExtension() is the same for 3 of the 4 strategies, but Risk
    Taker extends early
Problem: Rule-LK 5 lists "extend the loan period" as something a player
can do, but no strategy says when to actually use it.
What we did: Aggressive/Conservative/Opportunistic all share one rule -
only extend when loanRoundsLeft is 5 or less AND cash is less than the
loan amount, meaning default is a real risk. Risk Taker reuses his stated
trait "frequently refinances loans to increase available capital" -
extends any time loanRoundsLeft is 15 or less, even if he could otherwise
pay it all off, since his goal is more capital, not just avoiding default.

48. decideJailOrOut()'s jail release choice, different for every strategy,
    is fully made up
Problem: Rule 13 lists 3 ways out of Jail (pay bail, roll doubles, wait 3
turns), but never says which one a player should pick, and Section 3 gives
no strategy any Jail-specific behaviour at all.
What we did: split by the user's direct instructions. Aggressive
Investor/Risk Taker pay bail right away if they can afford it. Conservative
Banker only pays if his cash would still be at or above the average of all
4 players AFTER paying. Opportunistic Trader only pays while a Market Boom
(Rule-LK 30/31) is active, matching "always evaluates expected return."
Any strategy that doesn't meet its own condition rolls doubles instead
that same turn; if that also fails, the original rule still applies
underneath - pay forced bail after 3 failed turns - so nobody stays in
Jail forever.

game.c
========

49. The starting inflation rate is 0%
Problem: Rule-LK 12 lists the 6 possible inflation rates (-3%, 0%, 2%, 5%,
8%, 12%) and how often a new one is picked, but never says what the rate
is before the first one is ever generated, at round 10.
What we did: 0% was picked directly - it's already one of Rule-LK 12's own
listed values, so it needed no new number invented, and it reads as the
natural "nothing has happened yet" starting point. Set explicitly in
runGame() next to baseInterestRate's own starting value (#29), rather than
left as a silent side effect of GameState's zero-initialization in main.c.
