assumptions.txt
================
Judgment calls made wherever the assignment brief is silent, ambiguous, or
gives no formula/number, one per spot marked ASSUMPTION in the source. Each
entry: what's missing in the document, and what was chosen and why.
Organized by file, numbered in the order they were first written down.

Different from gapsFilled.txt (things that were broken and got fixed later)
and yetToMake.txt (things not built at all).


board.c
=========

1. Railway purchase price LKR 1,500 / mortgage value LKR 750
Gap: Appendix B only prices the 8 colour groups. Table 2 gives railway RENT
by station count, never a price to buy or mortgage one.
Chosen: mortgage = 50% of purchase, matching every priced colour group's
ratio. Purchase price set to LKR 1,500, same as utilities (#2), since both
are non-colour-group, flat/dice-based income sources with no stated price.

2. Utility purchase price LKR 1,500 / mortgage value LKR 750
Gap: Section 1.1.3 gives the utility rent formula only, no purchase price.
Chosen: same 50% mortgage ratio as #1. Purchase price matched to the
cheapest colour group (Brown), since utility rent is capped by a dice roll
rather than a group-scalable table - lowest ceiling, lowest price.


finance.c
===========

3. payRent() caps rent at the payer's available cash
Gap: Rule 7 doesn't say what happens if the payer can't afford full rent.
Chosen: letting cash go negative would break net worth/loan/auction math
elsewhere; forcing bankruptcy inside payRent() was rejected too, since Rule
14 compares a player's WHOLE position, not one payment. Capped at available
cash, leaving the real bankruptcy call to checkBankruptcy() (#6).

4. calculateNetWorth() subtracts mortgageValue as a liability
Gap: Rule 15's literal formula omits "mortgage liabilities" as a term, but
page 2's looser description names it.
Chosen: without an offset, a player could inflate net worth by mortgaging
everything and pocketing the cash while still counting full property value.
Subtracting mortgageValue treats it like an outstanding loan already is:
net worth reflects equity, not face value.

5. currentValue is one combined figure for "Property/Building/Railway/
   Utility Value"
Gap: Rule 15 lists 4 separate terms; Square only has one currentValue field.
Chosen: inventing 3 unused fields just to mirror the wording wasn't worth
it. One number per square, summed the same way regardless of type.
"Building Value" has no real backing since nothing raises currentValue when
a house/hotel is built (tracked in yetToMake.txt).

6. checkBankruptcy() triggers at net worth <= 0, returns every square to
   the Bank
Gap: Rule 14 promises "bankruptcy rules defined later," but no such section
exists - the exact trigger and transfer mechanics are undefined.
Chosen: net worth <= 0 already IS "liabilities exceed assets," so no second
formula was invented. Squares return to the Bank the same way Rule-LK 6
already handles foreclosed collateral, since no player-distribution rule
exists to follow instead.


events.c
==========

7. Heavy Floods' "coastal" reuses the same 6-square pool as #9/#10
Gap: Appendix A's Heavy Floods gives no square list for "coastal."
Chosen: two Regional Cards use the same word for named squares (#9, #10) -
reused that pool so "coastal" means one consistent set everywhere, not a
different guess per card.

8. Foreign Funding's "commercial property values" = every SQUARE_PROPERTY,
   board-wide
Gap: Appendix A names no board category called "commercial."
Chosen: Bank/Insurance/Utility squares have no currentValue to increase, so
a literal reading would silently do nothing. Applied to every property
square instead - the only squares that can meaningfully appreciate.

9. Beach Pollution's "Southern coastal rents" = Galle Fort, Unawatuna,
   Hikkaduwa (Yellow group)
Gap: Table 4 gives no square list.
Chosen: Southern Tourism Boom, same table, names these exact 3 squares as
"Southern" - read as the same region swinging two different ways, not two
unrelated unnamed sets.

10. Flood Damage's "low-lying coastal" = Bambalapitiya, Wellawatte, Mount
    Lavinia (Light Blue group)
Gap: Table 4 gives no square list; wording differs from Beach Pollution,
implying a different region.
Chosen: real, famously flood-prone Colombo suburbs, and a group distinct
from the Yellow group #9 already claimed.

11. Water Shortage's "surrounding properties" = Unawatuna (27) and
    Hikkaduwa (29)
Gap: Table 4 doesn't name what "surrounds" the water utility (square 28).
Chosen: read positionally rather than thematically - literally the two
squares adjacent to 28 on the board path.

12. Luxury Property Tax charges every round it's active, not once
Gap: Rule-LK 24 calls it "annual" but gives no count or stop condition.
Chosen: charges 25% of value every round via the round-tick system, for
GOVERNMENT_REGULATION_DURATION_ROUNDS (20 rounds) - the same duration
every other regulation already uses.

13. Disaster repair cost is tiered by disaster severity, off (currentValue +
    house/hotel replacement value)
Gap: Rule-LK 10 never gives a repair cost formula, and the 5 disaster types
have no stated relative severity to each other.
Chosen: user-directed tiering - Electrical Failure 5%, Riot 10%, Flood 15%,
Fire 20%, Building Collapse 30% (reuses DEPRECIATION_MAX_PERCENTAGE, the
document's own maximum-damage figure, for the most catastrophic disaster).
Applied to currentValue PLUS the square's houses/hotel replacement cost,
not currentValue alone, since a disaster on a developed property should
scale with what's actually built there. Compensation percentages
(80%/100% by policy) are unchanged - they still multiply this repairCost.

14. Business Interruption Insurance is treated as covering any disaster
    type (hotels only)
Gap: unlike Basic/Comprehensive, it lists no "Protect Against" perils.
Chosen: read as intentional - it protects the hotel's BUSINESS (lost
income), not a named peril, so it's peril-agnostic by design.

15. Building Collapse / Electrical Failure are covered by neither Basic
    nor Comprehensive
Gap: Rule-LK 10 lists both as disasters, but neither policy names them.
Chosen: implemented literally rather than assuming an oversight and
folding them into Comprehensive "to be safe" - matches the actual text.

16. A Government Regulation stays active for 20 rounds (its own draw
    interval)
Gap: Rule-LK 24 gives the draw cadence only, no duration.
Chosen: matches how Regional Cards (15 rounds) and National Cards already
tie duration to draw interval - keeps all 3 periodic systems consistent.

17. Disaster type and regulation selection are uniform-random
Gap: both just say "randomly," no weighting given.
Chosen: uniform is the zero-extra-assumption default; any non-uniform
weighting would itself be a bigger, unsupported invention.


players.c
===========

18. Conservative Banker's "bankruptcy imminent" = cash below 5% of
    STARTING_CASH (LKR 1,500)
Gap: Section 3.2 says "unless imminent" with no number.
Chosen: tied to STARTING_CASH, the one canonical baseline the document
gives. 5% keeps the line close to true desperation, matching this
strategy's risk-averse personality. Reused as the general "cash critical"
signal in decideMortgage() (#23).

19. Opportunistic Trader's "return exceeds borrowing cost" = current rent
    income vs. one round of interest
Gap: Section 3.4 names both terms but defines neither, and there's no
future-income projection mechanism anywhere.
Chosen: existing total rental income stands in for "projected return."
Borrowing cost reuses Rule-LK 4's own interest formula directly, the same
one finance.c already charges with.

20. Aggressive Investor's "funds that increase rental income" = a monopoly
    with room to build
Gap: Section 3.1 gives no income-projection formula.
Chosen: the only rule tying spending to more rent is building on a
monopoly (Table 6) - reused monopolyPlay() plus a house/hotel space check.

21. Risk Taker's "insures only after a loss" = "only once currently
    damaged"
Gap: the literal wording implies memory of a past event, even after repair.
Chosen: tracking permanent damage history would need a new persistent
field for one strategy's flavour text. Reused the existing isDamaged flag -
under-serves "after a loss" slightly, but keeps the reactive trait without
new state.

22. Opportunistic Trader's "high-value developments" = a hotel
Gap: no numeric threshold given anywhere.
Chosen: hasHotel is the one unambiguous "most developed" marker already in
the model (Table 6's top rent tier) - no arbitrary LKR cutoff invented.

23. decideMortgage()'s trigger and target choice is fully invented
Gap: Section 3 never mentions mortgaging for any strategy.
Chosen: strategy-neutral "last resort" trigger reusing #18's threshold,
rather than 4 unsupported personalities. Target = highest mortgageValue
eligible square, raising the most cash per property taken out of use.

24. Conservative Banker's "sufficient funds" for repayment = any cash > 0
Gap: "sufficient" isn't defined as full or partial.
Chosen: Rule-LK 5 explicitly sanctions partial repayment, so the full
loanAmount is always requested and repayLoan()'s own cash-cap naturally
turns it into a partial payment when needed.

25. Conservative/Opportunistic auction bids accept a bid == market value
Gap/context: Section 3.2/3.4 say "below"/"discount," implying equality
should be rejected.
Chosen: originally a strict "<", simplified to "<=" at the user's explicit
request, trading small literal accuracy for simpler code. Flagged here as
a deliberate, visible tradeoff rather than a silent one.


Added after the initial feature-complete pass, then simplified for the viva
================================================================================
Entries 26 onward were added once yetToMake.txt's remaining gaps (round-tick
systems, railway/utility rent, Income Tax, Economic Events, Rule-LK 36) were
filled in. An earlier version of several of these entries described more
intricate invented logic - notably an exact reverse-percentage system for
Rule-LK 35, and an Economic Events deck guessed from card names alone before
the real assignment text was available. Both were simplified afterward so
they could be defended confidently in a viva; the entries below describe the
CURRENT code. Kept as a trailing section rather than renumbering 1-25 above,
so "(see #18)"-style references keep working.

finance.c
===========

26. Income Tax = 15% of current cash (INCOME_TAX_STARTING_PERCENTAGE)
Gap: Rule 11 requires collecting tax on landing but gives no amount or rate.
Chosen: per the lecturer's clarification, set as a starting 15% rate rather
than a flat figure. Cash (not net worth/property value) was picked as the
base specifically to keep this tax structurally different from the
Community Development Fund tax (#42), which the lecturer bases on property
value - sharing a base would make the two taxes redundant. Stored as a
mutable Economy field so "Increase Property Tax" (+50%) has something to
change.

27. depreciateProperties() uses a plain modulo and a running
    depreciationPercent counter
Gap: Rule-LK 15/16 give the 1%-per-5-rounds/30%-cap rule, but not how to
detect "every 5 rounds" in code or remember how much has already been cut.
Chosen: propertyAge % 5 == 0 is the simplest timing check. A running
depreciationPercent field counts each cut and stops once it hits
DEPRECIATION_MAX_PERCENTAGE - one sentence to explain and defend. Cuts
apply to currentValue at that moment, stacking on whatever inflation/cards
already did to it.

28. Railway and utility rent are recalculated fresh at payment time,
    overwriting currentRent
Gap: Table 2 and Section 1.1.3 both define rent as computed live at
collection, not a fixed number like property rent.
Chosen: no card ever targets railway/utility rent directly, so there's
nothing to "build on top of." calculateRailwayRent()/
calculateUtilityRent() just compute the correct figure fresh every time -
one formula, no running total to keep in sync.

29. Starting loan interest rate = Table 9's "Stable Economy" rate (8%)
Gap: Rule-LK 2-7 describe how interest compounds, never what rate a loan
starts at, and nothing ties Table 9's 5 named conditions to a specific
moment in the simulation.
Chosen: rather than building a whole "which economic condition is it right
now" system, the game simply starts on the row every simulation naturally
begins on - Stable Economy, 8% - set once in runGame(), and lets
inflation/cards/regulations move it from there.

30. retryDamageRepairs() tells disaster damage apart from a Political
    Rally closure using closedRoundsLeft, not a separate flag
Gap: Rule-LK 11 says repairs "are automatically performed" once affordable,
but the original code only tried once, at the moment of the disaster. A
retry loop needed to avoid also charging a "repair fee" for a Political
Rally closure (#31), which should just reopen for free.
Chosen: closedRoundsLeft is already non-zero only for Political Rally, so
retryDamageRepairs() only attempts a paid repair where isDamaged == 1 AND
closedRoundsLeft == 0, leaving Rally closures to reopen for free instead.


events.c
==========

31. Political Rally reuses the isDamaged flag to represent "closed"
Gap: Appendix A's Political Rally closes a property for 2 rounds - a
different concept from disaster damage, but Square only had one boolean
for "can't collect rent."
Chosen: "closed" and "damaged" behave identically everywhere rent is
blocked, so a second flag would just duplicate every isDamaged check.
Political Rally sets isDamaged = 1 like a disaster, plus closedRoundsLeft =
2; board.c's reopenClosedSquares() counts that down and clears isDamaged
for free once it hits 0, independent of repair cost.

32. Insurance Discount grants an economy-wide -20% premium discount with
    no expiry timer of its own
Gap: Appendix A's card gives no duration or scope (all players vs. drawer).
Chosen: reused economy.insurancePremiumDiscountPercent, the same field the
Insurance Regulation already uses, rather than adding a player-scoped
field just for this card. It only turns back off as a side effect of a
Government Regulation's own timer expiring (see #33) - a small, accepted
inconsistency.

33. Regional Development Cards, Government Regulations, and Market
    Boom/Decline change prices/rent PERMANENTLY - none reverse when their
    duration ends
Gap: Rule-LK 35 says Regional Card effects should "return to normal" after
15 rounds; Rule-LK 31/32 imply the same for Market Boom/Decline. But no
formula exists for reversing a stacked percentage change (Rule-LK 34 lets
several effects stack on one square at once), and by expiry the same
square may have been changed further by other cards/inflation too.
Chosen: an earlier version tracked and exactly reversed each effect, but
needed a shared apply/undo lookup and careful division for a dozen
differently-shaped effects - too much to defend line-by-line in a viva.
Simplified to leave changes in place permanently, exactly like Inflation
and National Event Cards already work. The ActiveEffect itself still
correctly counts down and turns off (so the display and next draw are
correct) - only the price change itself is left as-is. Deliberate
accuracy-for-simplicity tradeoff.

34. The Rule-LK 33 group cooldown (30 rounds) starts at selection, not at
    the boom/decline's end
Gap: Rule-LK 33 doesn't say when the 30-round window begins.
Chosen: simplest to implement - set in the same line the group is picked,
no separate "effect just ended" event needed (which, per #33, doesn't
exist anyway). Since a boom/decline only runs 10 of those 30 rounds, the
practical difference is small either way.

35. The Economic Events deck (Rule-LK 18) skips three sub-effects that
    give no number to apply
Gap: of Rule-LK 18's ~14 bullet effects, 3 name no figure at all: Heavy
Monsoon's "Flood risk increases," and Political Unrest's "Riot probability
doubles" / "Business interruption claims increases."
Chosen: rather than inventing a fake probability system for these three,
they're left unimplemented with a one-line comment each. Every bullet that
DOES give a number is implemented literally. "Coastal"/"Commercial" reuse
the same square pools as #7/#9/#10/#8 for consistency.

36. Rule-LK 36's market conditions display prints once at the end of every
    round
Gap: the rule says to "display active market conditions" without saying
how often.
Chosen: there's no interactive input anywhere in the simulation (every
decision is autonomous), so "on-demand" has no trigger. Printed once per
round from endOfRoundProcessing(), alongside the existing round summary.


players.c
===========

37. Anti-Speculation Act's "3 undeveloped properties" counts only
    SQUARE_PROPERTY squares with 0 houses and no hotel
Gap: Rule-LK 24 doesn't define "property" precisely - unclear if railways/
utilities (never developable) or mortgaged properties count.
Chosen: railways/utilities excluded, since "undeveloped" implies something
that COULD be developed but isn't. Mortgaged colour-group properties still
count, since mortgaging doesn't reduce land-banking exposure. Checked at
the top of decideToPurchase() so it overrides every strategy uniformly.


game.c
========

38. Bankrupt players are excluded from the "who's behind" check that ends
    a round
Gap: a round ends when the SLOWEST solvent player completes a lap (not
fixed turn count), but a bankrupt player's lapsCompleted freezes forever
once playTurn() starts returning immediately for them.
Chosen: if bankrupt players were still counted, their frozen lap count
would become the permanent minimum, and round-end effects (interest,
inflation, disasters, every card deck) would stop firing for the rest of
the game. Excluded from this one check, consistent with how
checkBankruptcy() already excludes them elsewhere.

39. The round-boundary check runs after every individual turn, not once
    per 4-turn cycle
Gap: with round completion tied to board progress, the boundary can be
crossed by whichever player happens to be slowest, in any turn-order slot.
Chosen: checking once per 4-turn cycle could fire round-end effects a full
sub-cycle late. Checking right after every playTurn() call instead fires
endOfRoundProcessing() at the exact turn the boundary is actually crossed.
Side effect: substantially more individual turns are needed to reach the
500-round cap, since a "round" now means a full 40-square lap rather than
4 turns - the correct, intended consequence of this being real progress.


board.c (continued)
======================

40. Each property's purchasePrice/baseRent is hardcoded from Rent.csv,
    not read from the file at runtime
Gap: Rent.csv gives a distinct price/rent PER property, more specific than
Appendix B's one-price-per-colour-group table.
Chosen: a working fopen()-based CSV reader was built first, but file I/O
plus manual parsing plus name-matching was too many moving parts to defend
confidently in a viva for a 22-row file that never changes at runtime.
Replaced with the same 22 numbers typed directly into each square's
literal, the same way houseCost/hotelCost/mortgageValue already are.
Rent.csv is kept only as the reference the numbers were copied from.


players.c (continued)
========================

41. decidePayOffMortgage() mirrors decideMortgage() (#23) in reverse
Gap: like mortgaging itself, Section 3 never mentions paying one back.
Chosen: pays off once cash stays above the same #18/#23 danger line after
paying (cash - mortgageValue still >= 5% of STARTING_CASH) - one sentence
to state: "mortgage when broke, pay back once safely able to." Cheapest
mortgage is paid off first (opposite of #23's "highest first"), reclaiming
a rent-earning property for the smallest cost. Payoff = exactly
mortgageValue, no interest added back, since the document gives no figure
for one.


Added for the jail, maintenance-picking, and loan-extension work
================================================================================
Entries 42 onward cover judgment calls found while auditing decidePerform-
Maintenance(), decideRenovateStructuralDamage(), decideLoanExtension(), the
new-loan collateral choice, and the jail release logic - none of which had
been written down here yet even though each has an ASSUMPTION marker in the
source.

finance.c
===========

42. Community Development Fund tax = 10% of total Property-type currentValue
    only (railways/utilities/buildings excluded)
Gap: the lecturer's clarification says "10% of total assets... current
market rate of only the property (not buildings)," but "property" isn't
defined against Table 1's actual square categories.
Chosen: user-directed - read as Table 1's literal Property category,
excluding railways/utilities. "Not buildings" needed no extra work since
currentValue is already one combined figure with no separate building
component (#5). Kept on a separate Economy field from Income Tax (#26) so
the two taxes never share a base.

43. A new loan (takeLoan()) and increaseLoan() both pledge every eligible
    owned square as collateral, not a chosen subset
Gap: Rule-LK 3 describes Loan Locked properties but never says how a
player picks WHICH eligible properties to pledge for a given loan amount.
Chosen: pledges every unmortgaged, unlocked owned square, the same set
calculateMaxLoan() already sums to compute the ceiling - simplest possible
rule ("pledge everything eligible"), avoiding an unsupported partial-
selection algorithm the brief gives no basis for.

players.c
===========

44. decideToMaintain() is strategy-neutral for 3 of 4 strategies, but Risk
    Taker reuses his stated depreciation-neglect trait
Gap: Rule-LK 27 describes maintenance mechanically, but no strategy in
Section 3 names maintenance directly.
Chosen: Aggressive/Conservative/Opportunistic share one rule - fix the
lowest buildingCondition square they can afford, same as #23's reasoning.
Risk Taker extends his stated "ignores depreciation until repair becomes
unavoidable" (Section 3.3, written for a related mechanic) to maintenance
too: he only fixes a building once its condition drops to 25% (Table 3's
"Building Closed" cutoff), instead of at any condition below 100%.

45. decideRenovateStructuralDamage() is strategy-neutral: first affordable
    damaged property
Gap: Rule-LK 29 describes the renovation mechanically, but no strategy
mentions structural damage at all.
Chosen: same reasoning as #44 - picks the first eligible square the
player can afford the 25% replacement cost for.

46. decideRenovateDepreciation()'s Risk Taker (30% cap) and Aggressive
    (any depreciation) thresholds are invented
Gap: Section 3.2/3.4 give Conservative (>10%) and Opportunistic (>15%)
explicit numbers, but Risk Taker and Aggressive have no stated threshold.
Chosen: Risk Taker's "ignores depreciation until repair becomes
unavoidable" is read as waiting for the DEPRECIATION_MAX_PERCENTAGE cap.
Aggressive has no matching line at all, so it renovates as soon as any
depreciation exists, consistent with its rent-maximizing focus.

47. decideLoanExtension() is strategy-neutral for 3 of 4 strategies, but
    Risk Taker extends proactively
Gap: Rule-LK 5 lists "extend the loan period" as an available action, but
no strategy in Section 3 states when to use it.
Chosen: Aggressive/Conservative/Opportunistic share one rule - extend only
when loanRoundsLeft <= 5 AND cash < loanAmount, i.e. genuinely at risk of
default. Risk Taker reuses his stated "frequently refinances loans to
increase available capital" (Section 3.3) - extends any time
loanRoundsLeft <= 15, regardless of whether he could otherwise repay in
full, since his motivation is more capital, not just avoiding default.

48. decideJailOrOut()'s per-strategy jail release choice is invented
Gap: Rule 13 lists 3 ways out of Jail (bail, doubles, wait 3 turns) but
never says which one a player picks, and Section 3 gives no strategy any
Jail-specific behaviour.
Chosen: user-directed split. Aggressive Investor/Risk Taker pay bail
immediately if affordable. Conservative Banker pays only if his cash would
still be at or above the 4-player average AFTER paying. Opportunistic
Trader pays only while a Market Boom (Rule-LK 30/31) is active, matching
"always evaluates expected return." Any strategy whose bail condition
isn't met falls back to rolling doubles that same turn; if that also
fails, the original 3-failed-turns-then-forced-bail fallback still applies
underneath so no one stays jailed forever.
